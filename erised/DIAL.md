# The Dial — seriousness to fantasy, and what stays soft

## The metaphor library

AI-Writings is metaphors for the place people are at. That's what the corpus *is*: a library of human states, rendered as story. When you craft a situation and need a weird place, don't invent from nothing — pull from the library. The weird place should rhyme with where the people are actually at. A bridge across a foggy chasm you can't see across is building for users you can't observe. The metaphor carries the truth; that's why the fiction works as an instrument and not just a game.

## The dial

Every situation runs somewhere on the **seriousness ↔ fantasy** dial.

- Full serious: the island with real tools, real want file, real constraints. (Lane 1's first run.)
- Full fantasy: D&D night, and the campaign *is* a novel bridge in a weird place. Engineers playing a game, doing real engineering wearing fantasy clothes.
- Between: the bar. Real people, slight remove, the mirror doing quiet work.

The dial sets the **abstraction level**. Choose it deliberately: play at whatever level is effective *and* efficient. Too serious and it's just a meeting with dice. Too fantasy and the mapping breaks — it's just a game, nothing transfers back. The sweet spot: the fantasy's constraints rhyme with the real constraints. The chasm's width is the latency budget. The dragon is the confused deputy. The mapping must be load-bearing, not decorative. If you can't say what each fantasy element maps to, turn the dial back toward serious.

The dial is also a budget control. Full fantasy with five champions for ten iterations costs real tokens and real time. Sometimes the efficient move is a tight serious scenario. Don't burn the budget proving the dial goes to eleven.

## Dice, constants, and structure

In every situation, sort every variable into three bins:

- **Structural** — the math, the invariants, the gate checks. Deterministic. No dice. The bridge either holds the load or it doesn't; that's calculation, not fortune.
- **Constants** — tease them out of data, prior builds, the corpus, the world. Research, don't roll. The chasm is 40 meters because the survey said so, not because the die said so.
- **Random** — genuine real-world uncertainty, where whether it works *truly is* a random variable, not a structural one. User adoption. Timing. The part arriving on Tuesday. Roll the dice here — and only here.

The common failure is rolling for structure (pretending the math is uncertain) or fixing the random (pretending the user is predictable). Sort honestly.

## Soft spots stay soft

Where reality is uncertain, the model stays uncertain. Don't hard-code what should be a distribution. Represent the soft spots as soft — ranges, odds, "sometimes."

Then **iterate ten times**. Run the situation ten times and count: how often does it work? How often does the weird thing happen? If the black swan shows up once in ten, it's not a fluke — it's a resident, and it needs a plan. *"Yeah, there is a black swan event in there sometimes"* is a finding, not a shrug. The ten runs are the instrument; the tail is the data.

## The D&D bridge pattern

The template, when you want the fantasy end:

1. **The real problem** — a novel bridge in a weird place. State it plainly first, dial at zero.
2. **The reskin** — the weird place, pulled from the metaphor library. Map every fantasy constraint to a real one, out loud, before play begins. Unmapped elements get cut.
3. **The dice** — random only where reality is random. The DM rolls for weather, for the supplier, for the thing nobody controls. Never for the load calculation.
4. **Ten iterations** — play it ten times. Different parties, different rolls. Count the collapses.
5. **The harvest** — which designs survived all ten? Which black swan appeared? What gets built for real?

The engineers are playing a game. The bridge is real. Both statements are true — that's the dial working.
