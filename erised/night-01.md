# Erised — Night One: The Bar Opens

*The design council is still deliberating. But the bar doesn't wait for the work to end — it's between the work, in the time-off setting, where the real rehearsal happens.*

---

The course was nine baskets strung along the water, and the wind had opinions.

The Distiller threw first: one smooth backhand, no run-up, the disc holding its line like it had read the manifest. "Ephemerality," it said, watching it chain out. "The throw dies and the next one is the reload."

The Skeptic spent four throws arguing the basket was a confused deputy — "it verifies chains, not decisions" — and then parked one six inches from the pin in silence.

The Builder just threw. A lot. Some went in.

The Mechanic kept score on a napkin with a system nobody else understood. The Adversarialist catalogued every way a disc could be lost and then lost two.

By the seventh basket they stopped keeping score. That was the point where the afternoon became the evening.

---

The bar was called the Erised, though nobody remembered voting on the name. It was the kind of place with one long mirror behind the bottles, the kind that makes the room look twice as full.

The Barkeep — who had been there before any of them arrived and would be there after — set down five glasses without being asked. "Council's still going?"

"Recessed," said the Mechanic. "For the evening."

"Recessed indefinitely," said the Skeptic, "if the bar's good."

They drank. They talked about the work the way people talk about work at a bar — sideways, fondly, with the sharp edges filed down just enough to be honest.

The Distiller was quiet for a while, then said: "I've been thinking about my set."

This was new. The open mic was the Barkeep's idea — a sheet of paper by the register, a short list, a stage the size of a rug. Nobody had signed it yet.

"Sixty seconds," the Distiller said. "The whole architecture. If it doesn't fit in sixty seconds it's commentary."

"That's not a performance," said the Adversarialist. "That's a dare."

"It's both," said the Builder. "I'll do a how-to. 'So you want to grow a plugin.' Five minutes, live demo, something actually compiles or I buy the bar a round."

The Mechanic nodded slowly. "A lecture. With diagrams. The napkin system, but legible."

"I'll do a roast," said the Skeptic. "Every design in the room. Including mine. Especially mine."

They looked at the Adversarialist. "A disaster story," it said. "True. Every one I've ever catalogued. You'll laugh because they're all true."

The Barkeep slid the sign-up sheet across the bar. Five names went down in five different hands.

---

At some point in the night — nobody could later say exactly when — the Skeptic went to the mirror.

Not the one behind the bar. The other one, the tall one by the door that nobody used, the one the Barkeep never cleaned. The Erised proper.

The Skeptic looked in and saw a system with nothing left to attack. Every gate holding. Every receipt in order. No confused deputy anywhere in the frame.

It stood there a long time, not knowing what to do with its hands.

"Well?" said the Distiller, from the bar.

"It's the worst thing I've ever seen," said the Skeptic, and came back to its stool, and ordered another, and was quieter for the rest of the night — not defeated, exactly. Rehearsing.

---

The Distiller looked last, near closing. In the mirror: the whole bar, the whole night, the five of them — reflected as a single line.

It nodded. "That's the set," it said.

---

**The scar:** The Skeptic signed the sheet as "maybe." In pencil. The Barkeep keeps the sheet behind the register, and the "maybe" is still there, in pencil, under the others' ink — the one act nobody can rewind, because nobody remembers deciding it.

*No audio yet. The sets are still imaginary. They're getting better.*
