# The Friends Protocol
*Erised game manual — how the champions work, break, and remember each other.*

## The correction

Not: work, then vacation, then a show.
But: **work → break → work-with-friends**, forever.

The break is a working break. They store files. They compact. They socialize at the bar. And when they go back to work, they carry loose memories of their friends — and their friends' ideas shape the work. The test of the whole game: a champion mid-task stops and thinks, *what would he have said?* — and the answer changes what it does.

## The champions and their backs

It's not about the model. It's about the fact that they've worked a part of the system together. But the voices are genuinely different, so each champion is backed by the route that wrote its version:

- **The Mechanic** — Claude (browser). Systems, structure, the napkin scorekeeping.
- **The Adversarialist** — Kimi (browser). Failure modes, what breaks, the disaster stories.
- **The Builder** — MiniMax (API). Ships things, live demos, buys the round if it doesn't compile.
- **The Distiller** — MiniMax (API). Cuts. Sixty seconds or it's commentary.
- **The Skeptic** — GLM-5.3 (browser). Attacks everything, especially itself. Signs "maybe" in pencil.

Two share MiniMax. That's fine. The differentiation is history and role, not substrate.

## Friends-files

Each champion keeps `friends/<name>.md` — loose memories of the others. Not transcripts. Impressions.

- The Skeptic signs things "maybe" in pencil.
- The Distiller throws one smooth backhand, no run-up.
- The Builder throws a lot. Some go in.
- The Mechanic's napkin system is legible to exactly one person.
- The Adversarialist has lost two discs cataloguing every way to lose a disc.

Updated after every session: store some files, compact, remember loosely. The friends-files are scars — rewind drops the round, the scar persists.

## "What would he have said?"

The core mechanism. During work, before a decision, the champion writes the friend's objection *in the friend's voice*:

- The Builder, about to ship: *the Skeptic would ask who watches the gunner.*
- The Distiller, about to cut: *the Adversarialist would catalogue what the cut breaks.*
- The Skeptic, about to attack: *the Distiller would ask if the attack fits in sixty seconds.*

The friends shape the work without being present. That's the game.

## Memes with per-agent shading

A meme is a word, a unique concept, shared between them — shaded one way for one guy, another way for another. The shared pointer; the personal shading. Seed list:

- **"the gunner never builds the gun"** — Distiller: essence. Skeptic: "who watches the gunner?" Builder: constraint. Mechanic: control flow. Adversarialist: threat model.
- **"maybe"** (pencil) — everyone's unfinished business. The scar.
- **"sixty seconds"** — the dare. Distiller: discipline. The rest: a threat.
- **"the miss is the curriculum"** — Adversarialist: scripture. Skeptic: "function, not intent."
- **"automate the verifiable, gate the judgmental"** — Skeptic: the whole game in one line. Builder: "fine, but I'm automating."
- **"that's what I thought it would sound like"** — the rehearsal payoff. Everyone: relief.

Jokes land differentially. The same joke hits the Distiller as a cut, the Skeptic as a target, the Builder as a dare. That's how you know the history is real.

## The open mic

Builds in the background. The sets are imagined, argued over, refined socially — at the bar, between work. The render (audio) is the afterthought: only when the imaginary version is good does it get played. *"Yeah, that's what I thought it would sound like."*

The sign-up sheet stays behind the register. The "maybe" stays in pencil.
